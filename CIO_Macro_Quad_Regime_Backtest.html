How To Read This Page

1. Identify the macro regime.The scatter and monthly tape show whether activity and inflation pressure are improving or deteriorating. The four quadrants are intentionally simple: Goldilocks, Reflation, Inflation/Stagflation, and Disinflationary Slowdown.
2. Separate deceleration from contraction risk.The Downside Filter only activates inside the lower-left quadrant when at least two independent labor, activity, claims, payroll, or financial-condition flags agree. It is not a fifth quad and it never uses asset returns to define the state.
3. Translate to portfolio work.Use the sleeve and ETF tables as candidates, then require confirmation from the CIO Missive, Strategist command center, options, VAMS, and ticker tape before sizing trades.

Methodology

Regime definition: Growth Level is a balanced Macro Lens activity composite: Labor Market 16%, Credit / Liquidity 16%, Consumer Firepower 13%, Fiscal Monitor 10%, Manufacturing Cycle 14%, Housing / Construction 10%, Consumer 4%, NFIB / Main Street 4%, and Hard Real Activity 9%. Inflation Permission is intentionally excluded from this growth axis, so inflation is not counted on both axes. Growth and inflation impulses are two-month changes in four-month-smoothed Growth Level and Inflation Pressure, where Inflation Pressure equals 100 minus Inflation Permission. The four regimes use the signs of those two impulses. A 10% historical-volatility neutral band carries the prior direction only for a very small near-zero move; there is no persistence delay.

Model selection: the specification was selected from pre-set source, smoothing, neutral-band, and timing alternatives using independent FRED real-GDP and core-PCE outcomes. In the post-2018 holdout it classified the exact four-state outcome 46% of the time, identified the growth direction 62% of the time, and identified the inflation direction 70% of the time. A conservative one-month timing check was 58% for growth direction and 70% for inflation direction. Five- and six-state variants were rejected because they did not improve the full validation set consistently.

Macro downside confirmation: the four quadrants remain the primary model. Inside Disinflationary Slowdown only, the filter activates when at least two of five independent stress checks agree: Sahm-style unemployment pressure of 0.35 points or more, claims at least 20% above their 52-week low, industrial production below -3% at a six-month annualized pace, payroll growth at or below 1% at a six-month annualized pace, or a stressed/worsening Chicago Fed NFCI. The filter never uses ETF returns, SPY rebounds, or V-shaped-recovery exclusions to define a state. Every ETF is backtested from its own available confirmed-downside history; a fixed liquid proxy archive extends overlapping instruments to 2000 and independently validates the protection read. The recovery sensitivity audit discloses the price-rebound months that would alter the historical result, but those observations are never removed from the primary backtest.

Lower-left composition: the downside section reports what share of broad Disinflationary Slowdown months were macro-confirmed downside, price-relief recovery months, or ordinary deceleration. The categories are shown once per month for clarity, with macro-confirmed evidence taking priority if a month overlaps the price-recovery screen. The price-recovery slice is descriptive sensitivity only and never changes a macro label, a posterior probability, or a portfolio action.

Backtest definition: ETF performance is same-month return while the model is in the active regime. This says what historically worked during the same macro regime; it is not a standalone trade signal.

Observation rule: the four primary regimes use the selected minimum observation count. Downside Confirmed includes every available ETF overlap so the full universe remains visible; its evidence-weighted ranking automatically discounts short samples. Read any high-return table with observation count, hit rate, worst month, and bootstrap range before treating it as a portfolio signal.

Bootstrap definition: confidence ranges resample the same-month return observations inside each regime and estimate uncertainty around the average monthly return. The realized 5th-95th percentile range is shown separately as the historical volatility band.

Annualized versus average month: annualized return scales the average monthly return into a yearly pace. Average month is the raw observation and should be treated as the cleaner statistic when sample size is limited.

Data caveat: This uses the current Strategist ETF universe and available price history, so newer ETFs have shorter histories. Minimum observation filters reduce short-history distortion, but survivorship bias still exists.
