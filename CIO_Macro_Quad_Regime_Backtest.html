Historical Studies, Sleeve Candidates And Methodology

How To Read This Page

1. Identify the macro regime.The scatter and monthly tape show whether activity and inflation pressure are improving or deteriorating. The four quadrants are intentionally simple: Goldilocks, Reflation, Inflation/Stagflation, and Disinflationary Slowdown.
2. Separate deceleration from contraction risk.The Downside Filter only activates inside the lower-left quadrant when at least two independent labor, activity, claims, payroll, or financial-condition flags agree. It is not a fifth quad and it never uses asset returns to define the state.
3. Translate to portfolio work.Use the sleeve and ETF tables as candidates, then require confirmation from the CIO Missive, Strategist command center, options, VAMS, and ticker tape before sizing trades.

Methodology

Regime definition: Fast direction uses monthly changes without added smoothing. Growth weights are Labor 16, Credit 16, Firepower 13, Fiscal 10, Manufacturing 14, Housing 10, Consumer 4, NFIB 4, and Hard Activity 9. Fast growth uses only components present in both adjacent months, renormalized across that shared basket; at least 80% of configured weight is required. Changing coverage is flagged and raw level composition effects are separate. Unavailable months do not enter the historical study. Inflation Pressure is 100 minus Inflation Permission and is excluded from growth. Prior-only scales set neutral bands; neutral axes retain direction but contribute zero compatibility evidence. A top-two compatibility margin below 10 pp is a transition, not a confident regime. Structural context preserves the four-month mean and two-month change. Current states use newest cached monthly scores; only the historical study applies the assumed following-month-end release lag. No weekly observations or historical vintages are fabricated.

Model selection: source weights and thresholds are inherited research choices. Prior holdout percentages do not validate this changed timing convention and have been removed. No independent out-of-sample calibration is claimed.

Macro downside confirmation: the four quadrants remain the primary model. Every ETF is backtested from its own available confirmed-downside history; a fixed liquid proxy archive extends overlapping instruments to 2000 and independently validates the protection read. Backtest definition: Approximate release-lag forward study using today's revised caches. Observation months are assumed available at the following month-end; actual release dates and historical vintages are unavailable. Entry is the next month's first available close and exit its last close. Not vintage-certified or out-of-sample model validation. Rankings and selected-sleeve bootstrap ranges are in-sample descriptive evidence. Upstream score histories may be recalibrated from full-sample data; causal prefix checks here apply to unchanged monthly score inputs. Price boundaries use the supplied session index. Incomplete holding months and missing endpoint prices are excluded; transaction costs are not modeled.

Observation rule: the four primary regimes use the selected minimum observation count. Downside Confirmed includes every available ETF overlap so the full universe remains visible; its evidence-weighted ranking automatically discounts short samples. Read any high-return table with observation count, hit rate, worst month, and bootstrap range before treating it as a portfolio signal.

Bootstrap definition: confidence ranges resample the next-month holding-period return observations inside each regime and estimate uncertainty around the average monthly return. The realized 5th-95th percentile range is shown separately as the historical volatility band.

Annualized versus average month: annualized return scales the average monthly return into a yearly pace. Average month is the raw observation and should be treated as the cleaner statistic when sample size is limited.

Data caveat: This uses the current Strategist ETF universe and available price history, so newer ETFs have shorter histories. Minimum observation filters reduce short-history distortion, but survivorship bias still exists.
