How To Read This Page

1. Identify the macro regime.The scatter and monthly tape show whether growth and inflation impulses are improving or deteriorating. Disinflationary slowdown stays as one regime, with a separate V-shaped recovery adjustment for large equity rebounds.
2. Check whether the history is usable.The performance cards, bootstrap ranges, hit rates, and worst-month columns show whether prior returns were persistent or just one noisy episode.
3. Translate to portfolio work.Use the sleeve and ETF tables as candidates, then require confirmation from the CIO Missive, Strategist command center, options, VAMS, and ticker tape before sizing trades.

Methodology

Regime definition: Growth impulse is the 3-month change in smoothed Macro Lens Growth Permission. Inflation impulse is the 3-month change in smoothed Inflation Pressure, where Inflation Pressure equals 100 minus Inflation Permission. Goldilocks, Reflation, Inflation/Stagflation, and Disinflationary Slowdown are assigned from the signs of those two impulses.

Relief-rally adjustment: lower-left months are flagged as V-shaped rallies when SPY is up at least 6% after a prior-month SPY decline of at least 3%. The full lower-left sample remains shown, and the ex-rebound column removes those months to show the cleaner slowdown/stress read.

Backtest definition: ETF performance is same-month return while the model is in the active regime. This says what historically worked during the same macro regime; it is not a standalone trade signal.

Observation rule: each regime uses the selected minimum observation count. Read any high-return table with the observation count, hit rate, worst month, and bootstrap range before treating it as a portfolio signal.

Bootstrap definition: confidence ranges resample the same-month return observations inside each regime and estimate uncertainty around the average monthly return. The realized 5th-95th percentile range is shown separately as the historical volatility band.

Annualized versus average month: annualized return scales the average monthly return into a yearly pace. Average month is the raw observation and should be treated as the cleaner statistic when sample size is limited.

Data caveat: This uses the current Strategist ETF universe and available price history, so newer ETFs have shorter histories. Minimum observation filters reduce short-history distortion, but survivorship bias still exists.
